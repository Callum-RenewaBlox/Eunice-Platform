1 · How it works

Prices in, a power setting out

Each afternoon the day-ahead auction sets tomorrow's half-hourly prices. Eunice adds the network credit (gDUoS) and compares that export value with what the miners would earn. It then plans all 48 half-hours and sends Foreman one power setting per change.

Planned the day before. Decisions follow the day-ahead price, which is known by about 16:00 on the previous day.
Runs are commitments. A full start or stop has to last at least 8 half-hours, so Eunice only starts a run that pays over its whole length.
Safety is local. A hardwired trip drops the miners if the engine stops. Eunice sets the economics, not the protection.
2 · Three states, not two

Slowing the miners down beats switching them off

Miners are most efficient at lower power. The last few kW of overclock earn the least, so in many half-hours the best move is to drop to efficient mode and export what that frees up.

6 · Getting paid

SSP spill: paid the system price for every half-hour we export

Sites under 1 MW can't be their own Balancing Mechanism unit. Instead of an aggregator taking 25–30%, the export meter goes to a supplier who pays the System Sell Price (SSP) for each half-hour's metered export, plus gDUoS, less a small fee.

What we're paid

SSP × exported MWh
+ gDUoS credit (band rate)
− offtaker fee (target ≈ £1–1.50/MWh)

SSP is set after each half-hour from NESO's balancing actions. No volume to nominate, no penalty for over- or under-delivery.

Imbalance risk

With no contracted volume there's no imbalance, only price risk: exporting into a half-hour that turns out cheaper than mining.

Backtest: engine trips under a day-ahead-plus-SSP hybrid cost about £8–24 a year. The miners absorb every other swing.

Fees decide the winner

At ~2,500 MWh a year, each £1/MWh of fee ≈ £2,500/yr.

Ask for a fixed £/MWh, not a % of SSP (a % takes more exactly when we export into high prices). gDUoS at 100%.

7 · Next steps

What has to happen before Eunice goes live

Decisions and tests

  1. Fast trim. Who holds the site at zero import second by second? Foreman settles in minutes, so this needs a local controller on the boundary meter.
  2. Power-step test. One miner, 100% → 50% of installed in 5% steps, 30 minutes each: wall power, TH/s, temperatures. Confirms the 6.2 kW floor and whether lower is possible.
  3. Cost per start. A wear figure from the CTO lets Eunice price every start/stop.

Access and contracts

  1. Foreman Enterprise API key and docs. Confirm it uses MicroBT power adjustment (normal mode), not a preset mode switch.
  2. Modo API key for the GB EPEX half-hourly day-ahead feed.
  3. RFQ to npower, TotalEnergies, SmartestEnergy, Statkraft and F&S Energy for SSP spill on export MPAN 1170000388200.
